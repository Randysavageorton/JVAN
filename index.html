Choosing a machine

Which machine do I need?

Start with your product and your container, not a model name. Product, container and hourly output decide most of the choice. A thin oil, a thick sauce, a powder and a cream each need a different way of measuring the dose. A round bottle and a square bottle need different labeling. Send those details with a photo of your container, and our engineers name the machine that fits.

Link: Start with your product → /solutions/

Do I need one machine or a complete line?

It depends on where your process slows down. If bottles queue between stations, or you are starting a new operation, a complete line is the stronger investment. If one station lags while the rest keep pace, a single machine may be enough. Filling alone rarely helps when capping is still done by hand, because the queue simply moves to the next station. Share your current process and target output, and we recommend the option that suits them, including the single machine when it is the better buy.

Link: complete line → /complete-lines/

Can you build a machine around my bottle or jar?

Yes, and most orders begin this way. Send the bottle, the cap and the label with your product. Our engineers select the metering method, feeding system, capping head and label sensor around those samples. If a standard model already fits, we tell you so rather than quote a custom build you do not need.

Link: Send the bottle, the cap and the label → /about/how-we-work/

Can one machine handle different bottle sizes?

In most cases, within limits. A new bottle or cap size usually needs change parts, and some machines also need an adjustment to bottle handling. Send samples or drawings of each container before you order, and we confirm what has to change. If you run many sizes, say so in your inquiry so the changeover is planned from the start.

Link: change parts → /support/after-sales-service/spare-parts/

Do you make pharmaceutical process machinery?

No. We make packaging equipment for pharmaceutical and nutraceutical producers: capsule and tablet counting lines, bottle fillers, tube fillers and foil sealing. Tablet presses, granulators, coaters and blenders are outside our range.

Link: packaging equipment → /solutions/pharmaceutical-packaging/

Customization and OEM

Do you offer OEM and ODM?

Yes. We build under your brand and to your specification. The work ranges from a revised control-panel layout and machine color to a purpose-built configuration for a container no standard machine handles. Raise it at the inquiry stage, because most of what makes a custom build succeed is settled at drawing approval, before manufacture begins.

Link: drawing approval → /about/how-we-work/

What can be changed on a standard machine?

The usual changes are the number of filling heads, the capping head, the cap feeder, the label sensor, the contact material and the electrical build for your voltage. A clear label calls for a transparent-label sensor, and a corrosive product may call for SS316 in place of SS304. Stations such as date coding or check weighing can be added. Each change is confirmed against your samples before we quote.

Link: transparent-label sensor → /case-studies/perfume-spray-bottle-monoblock-usa/

Is the minimum order different for custom or OEM machines?

No. A custom or OEM machine is ordered one set at a time, like a standard model. If you expect a repeat order or several machines, mention it. The unit of sale does not change, but we can plan the build and the quotation around it.

Link: quotation → /request-a-quote/

Can a new machine work with equipment I already own?

Often, yes. Tell us the make of each existing machine and the height and direction of your conveyors. We assess whether the new machine can join your setup and what else would need to change. Where an older machine is better replaced than connected, we say that as well.

Link: join your setup → /complete-lines/

Quotation, price and MOQ

What do you need to prepare a quotation?

A finished specification is not required. Send what you have, and we ask for the rest. The details that help most:

  • The product, and its thickness at filling temperature, with any foam, solid pieces or corrosive contents
  • Bottle, cap and label samples, or photographs and drawings
  • Fill volume per container
  • Target output per minute or per hour
  • Floor space, local voltage and compressed air supply
  • Extras such as coding, check weighing or foil sealing

Physical samples give the most reliable result.

Link: quotation → /request-a-quote/

Can I order just one machine?

Yes. The packaging machine MOQ is one set. A single machine, or a single line, is a normal order. For spare parts and change parts, send the list and the quotation confirms quantities.

Link: spare parts → /support/after-sales-service/spare-parts/

How much does a machine cost?

The price depends on machine type, number of filling heads, level of automation, materials and customization. Where a product page shows a price per set, treat it as indicative for the configuration listed. Two machines with the same name can sit far apart once your product and bottle are known. Send your details and you receive an indicative price with a suggested configuration. If a simpler, cheaper machine meets your output and accuracy needs, we recommend it before the larger one. A formal quotation follows once the specification is agreed.

Link: indicative price → /request-a-quote/

Machine lead time and payment terms

Delivery dates and payment schedules decide whether a project fits your calendar and your budget. The answers below separate what is fixed from what your quotation sets for your order.

Prototype: order stages. Your quotation sets the dates.

How long does production and shipping take?

Stock machines dispatch within 7 days. Custom machines are built in 15 to 20 days. Shipping to your port is extra and depends on destination and method. A multi-station line takes longer, so the proposal gives one date for the whole line and states when the build period starts. The short build time reflects location: our factory in Wenzhou sits at the center of China’s packaging equipment industry, with components, tooling and fabrication close at hand.

Link: Shipping to your port → /support/shipping-lead-times/Link: our factory in Wenzhou → /about/factory-quality/

What can delay my delivery date?

Usually a change on the buyer’s side after the build has started: late drawing approval, samples that arrive once fabrication has begun, or a new bottle or cap. A revision at the drawing stage costs a conversation. After fabrication it costs time. Send samples early, and tell us if the container may change.

Link: late drawing approval → /about/how-we-work/

How and when do I pay?

Payment terms are written into your quotation and agreed before production starts. International bank transfer is the usual method for machinery export, and a letter of credit is possible when you request it. State your preference at the inquiry stage. Ask us to set the schedule against the order stages, which are drawing approval, build, test and shipment, so you can see where each payment falls.

Link: Payment terms → /request-a-quote/

Do I pay before I see the machine run?

The quotation answers this for your order. The machine runs your samples before shipment, and you receive photos and video to review, so you see the result before the machine leaves our factory. The payment schedule shows which payments fall before that test and which fall after it.

Link: before shipment → /support/shipping-lead-times/

Which shipping terms do you work with?

Tell us your destination port and preferred shipping terms, such as FOB, CIF or EXW, and the quotation reflects both. Machines travel in wooden cases sized to the equipment, and ship as complete assemblies wherever the design allows. Import duty and local taxes depend on your country, so your freight forwarder or customs broker can give you the figure.

Link: destination port → /support/shipping-lead-times/

Can I see the machine running before it ships?

Yes. We run the finished machine with your own product, bottles, caps and labels, then send photos and video for your review. Shipment follows your approval of the results, as set out in your contract. You are also welcome to attend the test at our factory in Wenzhou.

Link: attend the test → /about/factory-quality/

What do you measure, and what if the machine misses the target?

The test plan agreed with you defines what counts as a pass: the measurements, sample sizes, tolerances and acceptance criteria. Typical checks include bottles per minute over a timed run, fill weight or volume across a sample, cap tightness, label position and bottle-size changeover time. If the machine misses a target, we correct it on our workshop floor and run it again before packing. A fix in Wenzhou costs far less than a fix after shipment.

Link: what counts as a pass → /complete-lines/filling-capping-labeling-machine/

Can I watch the test remotely or send an inspector?

Yes to both. When travel is impractical, we run the test on a video call so you can watch live and request adjustments. You can also visit yourself or appoint a third-party inspection agency. Tell us who is coming and what they need to see, and we prepare the documents in advance.

Link: inspection agency → /about/factory-quality/

Filling machine warranty and spare parts

How long is the warranty?

The filling machine warranty is 12 months, the same as on every other machine we supply. The written terms state what is covered and when the period starts, and we send them before you order.

Link: written terms → /request-a-quote/?subject=documentation

When does the warranty start?

Your proposal names the starting event in writing, so you know the exact date from the outset. For an overseas buyer, the gap between shipment and commissioning can run to weeks, which makes this the first line to read before you sign.

Link: Your proposal → /request-a-quote/

What does the warranty cover?

The written terms list what is covered and what is excluded, and you can request them with your quotation. They also state how wear parts such as seals, nozzles and belts are treated, and who carries responsibility for damage in transit. Those two points deserve a careful read in any supplier’s terms.

Link: wear parts → /support/after-sales-service/spare-parts/

How do I make a warranty claim?

Message us the machine model, the serial number from the nameplate, and a photo or short video of the fault. For a filling machine, add the product name and its thickness. We usually reply within one working day, and WhatsApp is the fastest route. A video helps most, because our engineer sees the fault instead of interpreting a description.

Link: serial number → /support/after-sales-service/

Do you stock spare parts?

Stock differs by part, so each quotation confirms availability. Send the model, serial number and a photo of the part, and we reply with the part, the price and the shipping options. Ask for a spare parts list with your machine quotation, and we mark the parts that wear fastest so you can decide what to hold in your own store.

Link: spare parts list → /support/after-sales-service/spare-parts/

Is support available after the warranty ends?

Yes, through the same route: model, serial number and a photo. Parts and any site visit are quoted before work starts, so you approve the cost first.

Link: quoted before work starts → /support/

Installation and training

How is the machine installed at my site?

Most machines ship as complete assemblies wherever the design allows, so site work is positioning, connection and start-up, not rebuilding. How much help you need depends on the machine and your team. Your proposal states which services are included, which are optional and which you provide.

Link: start-up → /support/installation-training/

Do you send an engineer to my site?

For many machines, remote guidance is enough: a video call through connection and first runs, using your own product. For multi-station lines, or sites new to this kind of machine, an engineer can attend. Scope, dates and costs are agreed in writing before anyone travels.

Link: remote guidance → /support/installation-training/

Do you train my operators?

Yes. Training takes place at the factory test, by video call or at your site. It runs on your own machine with your product and containers, and covers start-up and shut-down, changeover between bottle or cap sizes, cleaning, routine checks and reading fault messages.

Link: factory test → /support/installation-training/

What should my site have ready?

Four things: your local voltage and frequency, the compressed air supply, a floor plan showing doors, columns and drains, and the height and direction of any conveyor already in place. Standard machines run on single-phase 110 to 220 V at 50 or 60 Hz, with 0.5 to 0.8 MPa of compressed air, depending on the model. Send these details early so the electrical and pneumatic build is confirmed before manufacture.

Link: floor plan → /complete-lines/filling-capping-labeling-machine/

Who helps me if I am outside China?

In Thailand, Greece, Slovakia, Chile, Brazil and South Korea, an independent local partner can help in your language and time zone. Elsewhere you work directly with our team in Wenzhou, by WhatsApp and email, on China Standard Time (UTC+8). The partners are separate companies, so ask each one which parts and services it offers for your machine.

Link: local partner → /about/global-network/

Company, documents and certification

Are you a manufacturer or a trading company?

We are a manufacturer. We make what we sell, and our engineers work in the same building as the workshop. You can verify this yourself: visit the factory, send an inspector, watch our machines run on video, or ask our partners about working with us over several years.

Link: visit the factory → /about/factory-quality/

Are your machines CE certified?

Our filling, capping and labeling machines hold six CE certificates, with separate EMC and Low Voltage Directive approvals for each of the three machine types. They cover those three types only, so ask which documents exist for any other machine. Copies are available on request before you order.

Link: six CE certificates → /about/factory-quality/Link: on request → /request-a-quote/?subject=certificates

What are the machine parts made of?

Product-contact parts on our filling machines are SS304 stainless steel, with SS316 available when your product requires it. We confirm the material in writing before manufacture. Tell us your product and target market at the inquiry stage.

Link: SS316 → /about/factory-quality/

What documents can I ask for?

We send any of these on request: CE certificates by machine type, a specification sheet with stated test conditions, a contact material specification for your product, dimension and layout drawings, test photos and video of your own machine, a packing list, warranty terms and company registration details. We build to the material and documentation specification you provide. Certification of your own production process remains with you.

Link: on request → /request-a-quote/?subject=documentation

No. This page describes how we normally work. Your quotation and contract state the terms for your order, and where they differ from this page, they take priority. Read them before you pay.

Link: contract → /request-a-quote/
